Chapter 05 · Methodology

The M4 Splitting Model

An online joint order-assignment, pod-selection and task-allocation model in which the decision atom is the item rather than the order — stated as the increment over M1 (Jiao et al., 2026).

One measured quantity runs the whole model. Travel distance, in metres. Every price in the objective is that distance divided by a different counter of work done — lines closed, orders completed, units picked — so nothing in the value side of the objective is a number somebody chose.

§1What changes, and what does not

M1 assigns whole orders. M4 keeps its resource layer intact and replaces only the order side. The list below is the complete difference.

M1 x̂o,w ∈ {0, 1}   the order is the atom   →   M4 q̂o,i,p,w ∈ ℤ≥0   the item is the atom

Splitting is not a feature bolted onto M1. It is what a finer atom makes representable: once a decision variable counts units of SKU i of order o drawn from pod p at station w, an order spread over several pods, stations or decisions is simply a feasible assignment of that variable.

Removed

(2) ∑w x̂o,w ≤ 1

(12)(13) δo,p,w

The one-order-one-workstation rule goes, and with it the auxiliary variable: qo,i,p,w already names the (order, pod, workstation) triple that δo,p,w existed to express. A finer atom does not only relax the model — here it also shrinks it.

Replaced

B1 qo,i,p,w ≤ q̂o,i,p,w

V1 ∑o q̂o,i,p,w ≤ n̂i,p zp,w

V2 ∑p,w q̂o,i,p,w ≤ ni,o

V3 ∑p,w q̂o,i,p,w ≥ ni,o êo,i

M1's (3) becomes B1, restated per unit. M1's single coverage inequality (5) becomes three: a draw needs stock on that pod at that station, never exceeds the residual demand, and a line counts as closed only when it is drawn in full.

Relaxed

B3 ∑o xo,w ≤ cw(t)

M1's (4) is an equality: assigned orders must equal capacity minus unused capacity, and the objective then charges 1000 per unused slot. B3 is an inequality, so a station may be left partly idle when nothing is worth sending. The unused-capacity variable uw disappears with it.

Added

V4 êo,i ≥ f̂o

B2 ∑i,p qo,i,p,w ≤ No xo,w

B4 xo,w ≤ ∑i,p qo,i,p,w

B5 ∑p,w qo,i,p,w ≥ ni,o eo,i

B6 eo,i ≤ êo,i,   fo ≤ f̂o

B7 eo,i ≥ fo

M1 needs no completion variable: under its coverage constraint an assignment already implies the order can be filled. Allow partial fulfilment and that implication breaks, so closure and completion must be carried explicitly — in both layers.

Unchanged

R1–R5 ← (6)–(11)

One pod per station, one robot per pod, one pod per robot, dispatch requires a robot, and pods and robots already committed by the system are inherited. Task allocation stays inside the model, exactly as in M1.

§2Two layers, and the rate between them

M1 already separates what could be assigned from what is assigned. M4 keeps that separation and prices the gap with a measured rate instead of a fixed weight.

The valuation layer is not slot-limited: it may score coverage the station cannot take this decision. The binding layer is what actually happens. In M1 the price ratio between the two is set by hand — α₁ = −40 on the valuation variable and, once the equality (4) is substituted into the objective, an effective −1000 on the binding one. That is an implicit realisation rate of 40 / 1000 ≈ 0.038, fixed for the whole run and for every instance.

β = cumulative bound lines / cumulative valued lines

M4 measures it instead. Of the lines the valuation layer scored as closed, what share did the binding layer actually take? That ratio is β, recomputed as the run proceeds and clamped to [0, 1].

Conditioned on committed supply

A single system-wide rate would be wrong, because the measured rate is not flat. On the small instance at 10 robots it is 0.2117 when one pod is already committed and 0.1376 when two are.

The cause is structural: slot capacity pins bound lines near 1.01 per decision whatever the committed count, while valued lines grow with it (4.79 → 7.38). The extra coverage a third pod is scored for is coverage the binding layer demonstrably cannot take. So β is conditioned on the committed-pod count, capped at three, with a stratum falling back to the global ratio until it has seen 50 valued lines.

A note on the symbol. This rate is written β, not δ, because the source paper already spends δ twice — as the capacity threshold that triggers a decision (p. 4, Table 5) and as the auxiliary variable δo,p,w (p. 6, constraints (12)–(13)). Jiao's symbols are kept as they are; this one is ours to name.

§3The objective is a ratio

Not a weighted sum of distance and value, but distance per unit of value delivered — which removes the need to decide how many metres an order is worth.

Only newly dispatched pods pay travel; a pod already en route or standing at a station has had its trip paid for and is sunk. Both D and V are in metres, so the ratio is dimensionless.

T6 — why the pod you drain matters

Which pod is actually drained has a real-world consequence, so only bound draws carry it. A unit taken from a pod being picked right now saves a future trip to fetch that item later, and is rewarded. A unit from a pod this decision dispatches genuinely costs an extra trip, and is charged. Queued and en-route pods are already sunk, exactly as in T1 and T2.

This term has no counterpart under an order atom. Reducing the decision atom does not only relax constraints — it opens a pricing dimension that did not previously exist.

Distance is the only thing measured. Each price is that distance divided by a different counter of work done.

β is not a price. It is dimensionless — the exchange rate between the two layers — and is the one quantity above that distance does not produce.

Why the ratios are not free parameters

The ratio objective is linearised by Dinkelbach, which solves min D − V at the current λ. That works only if V keeps its shape while λ searches — so μ, ε and ρ all scale with λ together, and only the level moves.

Their ratios are therefore fixed by the instance, not chosen: μ/λ is lines per order, ρ/λ is the reciprocal of units per line. If those ratios drifted with λ, changing λ would be changing the model rather than correcting a ratio estimate.

The warm-up ρ has to be a ratio, not a constant

It was once an absolute 15. Halving the warm-up λ to 5 then pushed ρ/λ from 1.5 to 3.0 — every draw-line move scored positive, no move was ever accepted, and the run produced zero orders. Pinning ρ/λ restores the invariant and leaves the fallback as what it should be: a starting guess for a ratio that the Dinkelbach iteration then corrects.

§5Fine print

Three things worth stating before someone else finds them.

“Zero hand-tuned constants” is not literally true

The four steady-state prices carry no tuning knob: every scale is 1 and both override fields are off. But some numbers are set by hand, and the honest claim has to name them.

ParameterValueWhat it governs
EpsilonScale0.001the whole run — but ε is only a tie-break, deliberately three orders of magnitude below λ
WarmupLines50when measurement takes over from the seed values
LambdaFallback10warm-up only
LinesPerOrderFallback2.4warm-up only
RhoFallback15warm-up only, as a ratio anchor
DeltaFallback0.05warm-up only

So: no price that governs steady-state behaviour is set by hand. The hand-set numbers are warm-up seeds and one tie-break scale.

λ's denominator includes work the picking decision does not control

λ and ρ divide the fleet's total distance, which also contains replenishment trips and pod returns to storage — roughly three quarters of it on the small benchmark. Detrended, λ correlates with replenishment intensity at +0.73 over two hours, and the sign is perverse: a busy replenishment period raises λ, which raises the value side, which makes the picking side more willing to dispatch exactly when the fleet is most contended.

An extract-distance denominator is implemented and switched off, because turning it on would move every published number. It changes λ's level only — μ/λ, ρ/λ and ε/λ share the numerator and are immune. This belongs in the limitations chapter, not the model chapter.

Splitting across stations costs two slots

Station capacity is still counted in orders — six on the small instance, inherited from M1 — and B2 makes any draw for an order at a station occupy one of them. An order served at two stations therefore consumes two slots. That is a structural reason why splitting concentrates in the temporal direction rather than the spatial one, and it is a property of the capacity semantics, not a defect of the model.
